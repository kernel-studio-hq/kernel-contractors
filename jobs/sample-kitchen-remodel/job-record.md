### What went well

- The hidden-damage exclusion and same-day photos made the subfloor change order an easy yes.
- Telling the customers early about the cabinet delay kept trust, even though they weren't happy.
- Installing around the damaged cabinet kept the counters moving.

### What didn't

- Cabinets were ordered after demo instead of at signing. That alone cost about two and a half weeks.
- Nobody checked the cabinet boxes against the order on delivery day; the damage was found at install.
- The failed inspection was avoidable. Nobody walked the counter receptacle layout with the electrician before rough-in.
- The soft floor was seen at the site visit, but the estimate didn't carry anything for it. The customer was told about it, which helped.
- We missed the promised finish date by three weeks. They had their visit, but in a kitchen without a backsplash.

### Lessons

_Copied to the [lessons-learned page](../../lessons/lessons-learned.md)._

- On any kitchen in a house this old, carry an allowance for subfloor repair under the sink and dishwasher, and tell the customer about it at the estimate.
- Order cabinets the day the contract is signed, not after demo, and check every box against the order the day it arrives.
- Walk the electrician through the plan for every new circuit before rough-in, and check box spacing ourselves before calling for inspection.
